# Field Tools promotional content catalogue

Created 3 November 2026 for Nic Payne. These are drafts to record or adapt, not published posts. Start with the review-delay demonstration: it makes the connection between AI, delivery, and caring for the people doing the work visible in one small example.

The offer: AI Stalemate helps a PM or lead developer prepare an AI conversation, explore where delivery gets stuck, and take a clearer question or proposal back to the team. Each edition contains its own Guide and conversation companion, plus the delivery map. A colleague can discuss exported notes without buying an edition. Product access belongs to the buyer.

The public sales pages are available for private reviewer invitations. Use purchase CTAs in public launch content only when checkout and the remaining launch checks are cleared. Reviewer discount codes are private and are not part of this catalogue.

## FT01 The code was ready Tuesday

**Estimated length:** 30–35 seconds at a conversational pace. Rehearse before recording. **Visual:** talk to camera, then show the three-part example on the catalogue. Label the situation “Fictional example” on screen.

“Say AI helps you finish the code on Tuesday. Great. But nobody reviews it until Friday. Then you find out you disagree about what it was supposed to do.

Where would you start?

AI Stalemate helps you prepare the AI conversation and look at the work around it: what held it up, what’s been tried, and what might help.

Pick one real piece of work. Leave with a question worth asking or a small change to propose. See what’s inside.”

**Screen sequence:** What happened → What to ask → What to try. Hold on “What would have had to move for the review to happen sooner?” before revealing the proposal. Do not portray the fictional result as a customer outcome.

**Destination:** [Field Tools](https://tools.rebel-leader.com/field-tools).

## FT05 Review delays post

**Draft**

“Can we please get reviews done faster?”

Before you ask again, take one recent change and look at what happened.

When was it ready for review? Who could review it? What were they already committed to? When the review finally happened, did you discover a disagreement about what the change was meant to do?

Those answers lead to different conversations.

You might need to make room for a reviewer. You might need smaller changes. You might need to agree one example of the expected behaviour before anyone starts coding.

AI may help with parts of that work. Following the work helps you decide where to look.

That’s part of what I’m building into AI Stalemate: help with the AI conversation, and a delivery map that helps you ask better questions about everything around it.

What happened the last time a change on your team sat waiting for review?

**Optional launch CTA:** “There’s a short example here: [Field Tools](https://tools.rebel-leader.com/field-tools#delivery-example).”

## FT06 Start on your own post

**Draft**

You don’t need everyone to agree to a new process before you can ask a better question.

Take one piece of work that got stuck. Write down what happened, what you think explains it, and what you still need to ask someone about.

“Reviews are slow” gives your colleague very little to work with.

“This change waited from Tuesday to Friday. You were already finishing the release. What would have needed to move to make room for it?” gives you somewhere to start.

You might discover your first explanation was wrong. That’s useful too.

I’ve designed AI Stalemate so one person can start, prepare their questions, and take their notes into the conversation. The other person doesn’t have to buy anything to talk it through with them.

## Additional content outlines

**FT07 Carousel — What would have to move**

1. “We keep asking for faster reviews.”
2. “Everyone is already committed to other work.”
3. “What would have to move to make room?”
4. “Try it with the next small change. Agree who can review and when.”
5. “Check the wait, rework, and the reviewer’s workload.”
6. “Keep, change, or stop the experiment based on what happened.”

Use an explicitly fictional example. Include “if review capacity is the issue” in the body: different causes need different responses.

**FT08 Teaching post — Does this decision need to happen today**

Open with a team waiting for a large decision when a smaller, reversible step may be available. Ask what must be decided now, what can wait, who is affected, and what information the small step would provide. Keep safety, access, and commitments intact. This is an optional way to handle a complicated decision, not a new checklist for every task.

**FT09 Longer teaching — What an objection is protecting**

Pair a PM’s opportunity with a lead’s concern about support, permissions, or engineering readiness. Ask what evidence each person has and what might change their view. Do not assume the objection is either resistance or wisdom. Finish with the smallest useful question they can answer together.

**FT10 Reviewer story — What changed after using it**

After a reviewer tries a real situation, ask what question or action they left with, whether they used it, and what happened. With their permission, publish the specific experience in their own words. Keep uncertain outcomes uncertain. Avoid generic praise, invented testimonials, or “saved X hours” claims without a defensible comparison.

## Message and recording notes

- Lead with a recognisable moment, show one question, then one useful output. A tour of every field makes the product feel heavier than it is.
- Say “AI might help” or “if coding gets faster.” Do not promise that AI halves development time or that all teams lose most of their time to bad process.
- Label invented timelines and examples. Do not use private employer material or identify colleagues in a promotional story.
- Respect both roles while allowing their assessments to be tested. “Both people deserve to be heard” does not mean “both are necessarily right.”
- Use “working faster without wearing people out” as a direction, not a measured performance claim.
- Prefer “a question worth asking” and “a proposal to discuss” to vague promises of clarity or claims that the tool fixes teams.
- Keep the Guide available as needed. Avoid selling completion, page counts, or a mandatory sequence.
- Suggested recording order: FT01, FT02, FT03, then FT04. Reuse FT04’s screen footage in the shorter cuts.
