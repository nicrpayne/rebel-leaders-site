## Buyer lens used for the review

- The buyer just watched Nic describe a frustrating situation, paid $47, and wants something useful for the next conversation. The welcome page gives one concrete first move.
- The landing page explains the situation, outcome, contents, example, and price with short copy. Teaching happens in the paid Guide and Lab.
- The voice speaks PM to PM, acknowledges legitimate stakeholder constraints, and avoids casting colleagues as villains.
- Each tool teaches the buyer to connect the work to a worthwhile customer or team outcome and invite the other person to shape the future.
- A proposal remains a proposal until the buyer records an actual decision. Lab examples start before agreement; Guide examples show the subsequent fictional decision and follow-through. The reader is told which stage the Lab example starts at.
- Each Lab sequences one situation and creates three records that can be used in the organization's existing tools. Private reactions and motive assumptions stay out of every export.
- Capacity arithmetic is optional, explicitly based on entered person-days, and does not produce a release date or probability. Missing estimates remain unknown.
- Decision rights maps are unconfirmed until verified. A Reset Request grants no authority and does not waive specialist approvals.
- The Translator checks the stakeholder's underlying need rather than mind-reading it. It acknowledges that sufficiently understood delivery work can support real planning commitments.
- Drafts are local to the device/browser. Copy, text download, and print are the supported transfer routes; no cloud-sync promise is made.

## Research interpretation

Seven supplied papers were read from the attached local copies. They inform editorial choices, not claims that these products are validated leadership interventions.

- Campbell, *On the journey toward wholeness in leader theories* (2007): conceptual holistic model. Used to keep values, emotion, behavior, and relationships in view alongside an operating decision. Not treated as evidence that one worksheet changes leadership outcomes.
- Afrianty, *Are Leaders Born Or Made? A Point Of View* (2020): explicitly a point of view/literature discussion. Used as background for practice and learning; not presented as proof of a particular innate/learned split.
- von Krosigk, *A holistic exploration of leadership development* (2007): context-specific qualitative exploration. Used as background for attention to organizational context and self-development; broad causal claims are avoided.
- Boyatzis, Smith, and Blaize, *Developing Sustainable Leaders Through Coaching and Compassion* (2006), DOI 10.5465/AMLE.2006.20388381: conceptual integration with propositions the authors say require longitudinal testing. Used to favor curiosity and supportive coaching; no physiological or health benefits are claimed.
- Goldman, Wesner, and Karnchanomai, *Reciprocal Peer Coaching: A Critical Contributor to Implementing Individual Leadership Plans* (2013): study of a particular medical/health-science faculty setting with implementation support. The authors caution about generalizability and inability to isolate each support component. Used to suggest optional peer rehearsal, action, and reflection; no quantified performance benefit is claimed.
- Prati, McMillan-Capehart, and Karriker, *Affecting Organizational Identity: A Manager's Influence* (2009), DOI 10.1177/1548051809331502: theorizes emotional intelligence, coordination, and identity. Used as background for shared outcomes and coordination; not treated as empirical validation of these instruments.
- Bass, *Two Decades of Research and Development in Transformational Leadership* (1999): research review connecting desirable futures, intellectual stimulation, and individual development. Used as background for practical shared vision and authorship; no career outcome is guaranteed.

The official Scrum Guide and Agile Manifesto principles were also checked for delivery planning, quality, sustainable work, and adaptation: https://scrumguides.org/scrum-guide.html and https://agilemanifesto.org/principles.html. The tools are context-specific practices, not replacements for the frameworks or required engineering controls.
